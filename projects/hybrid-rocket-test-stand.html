03 / NITTANY ROCKET LABS · PROPULSION TEST INFRASTRUCTURE

Hybrid Rocket Motor Test Stand.

Designed a modular, load-cell-equipped test stand for Nittany Rocket Labs to support hybrid rocket motors up to 8 inches in diameter and up to 2.5 kN of thrust. After the initial structural analysis identified a critical failure location, I revised the load-bearing structure and evaluated the updated design in FEA under a 5 kN applied load.

Project objective

The objective was to develop a reusable, modular structural platform for hybrid rocket motor testing. The stand was designed around a clamping interface capable of accommodating a motor up to 8 inches in diameter. Its modular arrangement was intended to support multiple engine configurations while providing a defined structural path for transferring thrust loads to a load cell.

The load cell is a central part of the test-stand concept: it allows thrust to be measured through the stand's structural load path rather than treating the frame as a simple motor holder. The structure therefore had to satisfy two connected goals—securely support the motor at the clamp interface and carry the resulting test load through the stand's supporting members and measurement arrangement.

DESIGN ENVELOPE

Motor interface: hybrid rocket motor up to 8 in. in diameter
Intended motor output: up to 2.5 kN thrust
Structural simulation: redesigned stand evaluated under a 5 kN applied load
Test objective: modular motor support with thrust measurement through a load cell

Initial design and FEA findings

The first test-stand configuration was modeled and evaluated using Finite Element Analysis. The initial FEA identified a critical failure location under the maximum applied loading condition. This indicated that the original load-bearing geometry did not provide the structural performance required for the intended test-stand application.

Rather than treating the initial model as complete, I used the analysis as a design feedback loop. The stress and failure indications highlighted the need to revise the members carrying the primary structural load. The initial configuration therefore became the baseline for a second design iteration.

Structural redesign

The load-bearing members were re-engineered with expanded cross-sections to address the stress concentrations identified in the first analysis. The revised structure retained the overall purpose of the modular test stand while increasing the structural capacity of the members identified as critical in the initial FEA.

This redesign demonstrates how simulation can inform a practical engineering decision: identify the region governing the response, modify the geometry of the affected structural members, and return to analysis to evaluate the updated configuration against the intended load case.

5 kN FEA evaluation

The redesigned stand was simulated under an applied force of 5 kN. The updated FEA indicated that the revised structure could withstand this modeled load and met the project's stated structural-compliance objective prior to fabrication.

For context, the intended hybrid rocket motor can produce up to 2.5 kN of thrust. The 5 kN FEA load is therefore twice the stated maximum motor thrust:

5 kN ÷ 2.5 kN = 2.0

This is a comparison between the modeled applied load and the motor's stated maximum thrust—not a claim of a certified factor of safety. The FEA result is specific to the model, its assigned material properties, geometry, constraints, and loading assumptions.

Engineering workflow

  1. Define the application. Establish the motor interface envelope, including a maximum 8 in. motor diameter, the 2.5 kN maximum thrust target, and the need to measure thrust with a load cell.
  2. Develop the modular structure. Model a test-stand arrangement intended to accommodate multiple motor configurations and provide a structural path for test loads.
  3. Evaluate the initial design. Use FEA to review the frame's structural response under the applied loading and identify the critical failure location.
  4. Revise the load-bearing members. Expand the cross-sections of the members associated with the critical response to address the stress concentrations found in the initial model.
  5. Re-run the analysis. Evaluate the improved configuration under a 5 kN applied load and compare the modeled structural response with the project's design objective.

Engineering considerations

This project brought together structural analysis and the requirements of a propulsion test environment. Key considerations included the clamp-to-frame interface, the path by which thrust is transferred through the structure, the placement of a load cell within that path, and the need to preserve modularity for different engine configurations.

The FEA-driven redesign also reinforced the importance of evaluating the complete structural load path. A localized weakness can govern the behavior of the overall stand, even when other members appear sufficiently robust. The revised model addressed the critical regions identified in the first analysis by increasing the cross-sections of the load-bearing members.

Outcome and scope

The final design iteration was evaluated in FEA under a 5 kN applied load—twice the intended motor's maximum stated thrust of 2.5 kN. The analysis supported the revised stand's structural compliance for the planned test application before fabrication. The design also retained the intended capacity to clamp around a hybrid rocket motor up to 8 inches in diameter and to measure thrust through a load cell.

Analysis scope: the 5 kN result is a simulation outcome, not a physical proof-load result. The project material documents the stand's design and FEA iterations before fabrication; it does not establish completed fabrication, physical load testing, or a completed hot-fire test.

ENGINEERING TAKEAWAY

This project demonstrates an iterative structural-design process: translate a propulsion-test requirement into a mechanical support concept, use FEA to identify a governing structural weakness, modify the load-bearing geometry, and evaluate the redesigned structure under a defined 5 kN load case.
