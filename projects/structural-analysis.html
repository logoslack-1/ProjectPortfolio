03 / STRUCTURAL MECHANICS

Thin-Walled Section Analysis.

Applying mechanics of materials to understand how thin-walled structural sections carry transverse shear and develop shear-flow distributions.

MECHANICS OF MATERIALSSHEAR FLOWSECTION PROPERTIES

Project overview

This academic analysis explores the behavior of thin-walled cross-sections under transverse loading. The focus is on calculating section properties, determining the shear-flow distribution through individual wall segments, and understanding how geometry influences structural response.

CORE RELATIONSHIP

q = VQ / I

For thin-walled sections, shear flow is evaluated along the wall contour using the appropriate first moment of area and the relevant second moment of area for the loading direction.

Analysis workflow

  • Define the cross-section geometry, wall thicknesses, and coordinate reference.
  • Calculate centroid location and second moments of area about the required axes.
  • Divide the section into wall segments and establish a consistent contour direction.
  • Evaluate the first moment contribution accumulated along each wall to determine shear flow.
  • Apply boundary conditions at free edges and enforce flow continuity at junctions.
  • Check force equilibrium and, where relevant, determine shear-center behavior.

Engineering concepts

Centroids, area moments of inertia, unsymmetrical bending, transverse shear, thin-walled beam theory, shear center, and equilibrium of open and closed sections.

Why it matters in aerospace

Thin-walled structures are common in aircraft and launch-vehicle construction because they provide efficient load paths with relatively low mass. Understanding shear flow is essential when assessing webs, skins, stiffeners, and other structural members.
