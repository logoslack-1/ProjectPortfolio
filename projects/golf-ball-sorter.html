Automated Golf Ball Sorter.

A microcontroller-based sorting concept that combines optical sensing, RGB color classification, and servo-driven mechanisms to route golf balls by color.

ARDUINO UNOCOLOR SENSINGSERVO ACTUATION

Project overview

The goal is to create a compact, low-cost mechanism that detects an approaching golf ball, identifies its color, and actuates gates to direct it into the appropriate path. The design combines sensor timing, mechanical sequencing, and embedded control.

SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

Ball detection → color measurement → classification → timed servo actuation → discharge and reset.

Hardware and control

  • Arduino Uno R3 as the central controller.
  • Three MG996R servos assigned to the entry gate, color gate, and sorting arm.
  • TCRT5000 reflective optical sensor used to detect ball presence.
  • RGB color sensor readings used to distinguish red, blue, and white balls.
  • Timed gate sequencing and a lockout interval to reduce repeated triggers while a ball passes through the mechanism.

Control logic

The controller waits for a valid object-detection event, acquires color readings, classifies the ball using calibrated channel ranges, and moves the sorting arm to the corresponding position. A delay between detection and actuation allows the ball to reach the sorting gate. The mechanism then returns to its initial state.

Engineering considerations

Sensor calibration, ambient-light sensitivity, servo torque, mechanical friction, ball spacing, timing repeatability, and safe power distribution are important design constraints. Servo current demand should be supplied by an appropriately rated external supply with a common ground to the controller.

Next design iterations

  • Build a rigid frame and low-friction ball guide to improve repeatability.
  • Enclose the color sensor to reduce ambient-light variation.
  • Log sensor readings and classification outcomes for calibration.
  • Test throughput, misclassification rate, and jam resistance under repeated operation.
